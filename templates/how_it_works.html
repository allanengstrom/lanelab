HOW LANE LAB WORKS

What happens between hitting Run Optimizer and getting a lineup: the data, the opponent model, the simulation, and the search.

1The data

Everything starts with five seasons of NVSL results (2021–2025) collected from mynvsl.com: every swimmer's time in every event, plus each team's full lineup history. You can add your team's SwimTopia roster (the official current-season roster) and a HY-TEK time-trial PDF, which covers new swimmers without race history.

2Swimmer profiles

For every swimmer and stroke, the app builds a profile: a typical time, and how much that time varies from race to race. Consistency differs widely between swimmers, and the optimizer uses both numbers.

Returning swimmers who haven't raced yet this season get estimated times from last year's results: the app takes where they ranked relative to the field last year and carries that standing forward, adjusted for being a year older. You review this list during setup and can drop anyone who left the team.

3Predicting the opponent

A prediction model trained on thousands of real NVSL lineup decisions estimates what the other coach will run. It uses their roster, each swimmer's results, and that coach's historical patterns to pick the lineup they're most likely to swim.

The model predicts the opponent's exact lineup 53% of the time, compared with 38% for the simpler approach it replaced. The scenario mix in section 5 covers the remaining uncertainty.

4Simulating a meet

Lineups are compared by simulation. During the search, each candidate lineup is scored over 3,000 simulated meets; the final numbers shown on the results page use 10,000. In each simulated meet, every swimmer gets a race-day time drawn from their profile (typical speed, plus or minus their usual variation), every event is scored 5-3-1 per NVSL rules, relays included, and the points are totaled.

Every candidate lineup is tested against the same pre-drawn sets of race-day variation (a technique called common random numbers), so a half-point difference between two lineups is a real difference rather than simulation noise. Repeated event matchups are cached, which makes the search roughly ten times faster.

5Hedging the bet

Optimizing against the single predicted lineup would be fragile, so every candidate is scored against a weighted mix of scenarios: the opponent's predicted lineup (70%), their best counter to your last-week lineup (15%), and their best counter to your most obvious lineup (15%).

The weights adapt to the opponent, based on five years of their lineup behavior. Highly consistent coaches shift weight toward the predicted lineup, up to 85%. Coaches who reshuffle frequently shift weight toward the counter scenarios, up to 25% each. A lineup has to score well across the whole mix, not just the most likely case.

6The search

A legal lineup follows league rules: at most 2 individual events per swimmer, at most 3 swimmers per event, and age eligibility for every entry (swimming up into an older group is allowed). That leaves far more possibilities than can be tried exhaustively.

The optimizer starts from the best "blind" lineup and improves it by local search: try a swap, re-simulate, keep the change if it raises the expected win probability across the scenario mix, and repeat until no swap improves the result. This search is what runs during the 60–90 second optimize.

7Keeping the number honest

Raw simulation output is overconfident, because the lineup was optimized inside the model's own view of the world. The headline win percentage is therefore calibrated: compared against how past predictions actually turned out, and adjusted until the stated probabilities match real outcomes.

Week 1 is the exception. With no current-season races, lineup prediction accuracy drops to roughly a third, so Week 1 uses its own calibrated estimate and is labeled as a rough early-season guide rather than a confident forecast.

8What it can't know

The model has no knowledge of absences (you mark those in setup), swimmers with no recorded races (partially covered by time-trial uploads), disqualifications, or off-season roster changes on other teams. The Check page exists for applying information the model lacks.
